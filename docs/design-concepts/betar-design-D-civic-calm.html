Legal and policies

Privacy

Betar never asks for a phone number or an email address. There is no server, so there are no server logs of who you talked to or what you posted.

Location is only ever shared when you tap the share button in a chat, once, for that message alone.

Terms

Betar is free and open source, licensed under AGPL-3.0. Anyone can read the code, change it, or run their own version.

There is no company behind this app and no account to agree to a contract with. Using Betar means agreeing to carry and relay other people's encrypted messages as part of the mesh.

Content policy

No one, including the people who built Betar, can delete a post or message once it has spread across the mesh or the relay network. There is no server to remove it from.

Each person's own app can locally hide or warn on content flagged through Community Notes. This runs on your device only and never removes anything for anyone else.

How Betar works

Local mesh

Your phone talks directly to nearby phones over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi. No internet needed. Messages hop from phone to phone to reach further than your own radio range.

Relay bridge

The moment any one phone nearby reaches the internet, even briefly, it bridges everything to the open relay network and back. You never need your own internet, only someone near you, eventually, does.

Feed rooms

Posts live in location-scoped rooms, for example #oldtown or #citycenter, not one giant global feed. Chronological only. Nothing is ranked or boosted by an algorithm.

Community notes

Anyone can attach a note to a post. A public, shared scoring method decides which notes rise, not a moderator team, so no single person decides what counts as true.

Your ID

A short code like A3F9K2, generated from your device's private key the first time you open Betar. It cannot be changed, and it is not tied to your name, phone number or email.

You can also set a display name in Profile, 3 to 8 characters, letters and digits plus . and _ only. It is a nickname only, shown next to your ID, and is not guaranteed to be unique to you.

Panic wipe

Erases everything on this device and retires your ID for good. Opening Betar again after that starts over completely, with a brand new ID. The same thing happens if you uninstall and reinstall the app.

How to read this mockup

  • Tap the bottom tab bar to switch between Feed, Nearby, Chats and Profile.
  • Tap a post with a Community note badge, or any chat row, to open its detail screen.
  • Feed posts carry a geohash room tag (for example #oldtown, city precision) instead of one undifferentiated global feed.
  • The round button on Feed starts a new post, including an optional image. A mesh-only image stays a small preview until a relay bridge is reached.
  • The pin button in a chat thread sends a one time location, not a continuous live share.
  • Nearby shows local mesh peers as a small network graph plus relay bridge status in plain language.
  • Profile carries the panic wipe control, kept visually distinct and separate from ordinary settings.
  • Onboarding shows the auto-generated ID (for example A3F9K2) the moment it exists. It is permanent on this device, derived from the real private key, not a separately issued number.
  • The display name next to the ID (for example rupa.k) is an editable nickname only, 3 to 8 characters, letters and digits plus . and _. It is not guaranteed unique, since no server exists to check that.
  • "How it works" opens from onboarding or from Profile and explains the mesh, the relay bridge, feed rooms, Community Notes and the ID system in plain language.
  • Panic wipe and an uninstall-reinstall both end the same way: everything on the device is gone, and the next launch creates a brand new ID from nothing.
  • Posts and comments are capped at 280 characters.
  • A Community Note needs at least 2 source links plus a 150 character explanation before it can be submitted, shown on the post detail screen.
